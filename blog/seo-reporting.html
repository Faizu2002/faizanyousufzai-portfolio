SEO Reporting: Results, Changes & Next Actions

Create clearer SEO reports with trends, page and query changes, KPI context, annotations, business outcomes and a prioritized action plan.

By Faizan Khan YousufzaiUpdated October 4, 20268 min read
Quick answer

An SEO report should say what changed, where it changed, why it may have changed, what is uncertain and what action follows. Metrics are evidence, not the report itself.

A monthly report can contain accurate numbers and still be useless if the reader cannot tell what happened or what should happen next.

Lead with the outcome and context

Start with the major movement in organic search and the date range. Mention major site changes, algorithm events or seasonality only when they are relevant and supported.

Avoid celebrating percentage growth from tiny baselines without showing the absolute numbers.

Break change down by pages and queries

If clicks moved, identify which URLs and queries explain the difference. This separates a sitewide issue from a small cluster problem.

Country, device and search appearance filters can add context when they materially change the interpretation.

Separate facts from hypotheses

The report can state that impressions fell on a group of pages. The reason may still be a hypothesis until further checks confirm it.

Label assumptions clearly and list the evidence needed to validate them.

Connect performance to work completed

Record important SEO changes such as new clusters, migrations, internal linking updates or technical fixes. This creates a history for later comparisons.

Do not claim causation just because a metric improved after a change. Use careful language and multiple signals.

Finish with a prioritized action list

The report should end with the few actions most likely to improve or protect performance. Include owners and expected checks where possible.

A short, specific list is more useful than repeating every audit recommendation each month.

A simple SEO report template

A strong SEO report can be organized into five parts: executive summary, visibility trend, page and query drivers, business outcomes, and next actions. That structure works for an internal team or an SEO client report because it begins with meaning before showing detail. Screenshots and charts should support the explanation, not replace it.

For page and query analysis, show what actually caused the movement. If clicks increased because one guide earned new impressions, say that. If sitewide impressions fell but a key commercial group improved, separate those stories. This makes SEO analytics reporting more useful than a single percentage change.

What makes an SEO report credible

  • Use absolute numbers with percentages so small baselines are not exaggerated.
  • Separate facts from hypotheses about why a change occurred.
  • Include relevant implementation history, such as a migration or new cluster.
  • Avoid claiming causation when timing is the only evidence.
  • Finish with two to five prioritized actions tied to the SEO strategy.

A sample SEO report should make the reader confident about what happened and what happens next. More pages in the report do not automatically create more insight.

Common questions

What should be in a monthly SEO report?

Key outcomes, important page and query changes, relevant technical or content updates, business metrics and a short next-action list.

Should reports include keyword rankings?

They can, but Search Console page and query data often provides broader context than a small tracked keyword set.

How do I explain SEO results to a non-SEO reader?

Lead with business impact and simple trends, then provide technical detail only where it explains the result or decision.

Related Search Console update: If Google Search Console clicks and impressions stop updating, avoid presenting missing dates as zero traffic. This guide to delayed Search Console Performance data explains how to check report freshness and document the issue for clients.

Final takeaway

Good SEO reporting reduces uncertainty. Show the movement, isolate the pages and queries behind it, separate evidence from assumptions and end with the next decision.

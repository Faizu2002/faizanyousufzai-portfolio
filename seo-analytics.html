SEO Analytics

SEO analytics that explains what changed and what to do next.

SEO analytics should answer useful questions: which pages are gaining demand, which queries are close to stronger positions, where CTR is weak, and whether traffic supports the site's goals.

Measure search visibility before conversions

Search Console shows impressions, clicks, CTR and average position. Those metrics explain what happens in Google Search before a visitor lands on the website.

Analytics tools explain what happens after the visit. The numbers do not match perfectly because the tools measure different events.

Use segments to find the real change

Sitewide totals can hide the cause of a gain or decline. Break performance down by page, query, country, device and time period before making changes.

A small group of URLs often explains a large share of movement, which makes the next action clearer.

Report decisions, not only metrics

A useful SEO report should connect movement to pages, queries, changes and next steps. Metrics without context create activity but not direction.

I prefer a short list of high-confidence actions over a dashboard full of numbers that no one uses.

How this topic connects to the rest of SEO

Analytics closes the SEO loop. Research and implementation create changes; Search Console and analytics data show whether those changes improved discovery, clicks and useful visits.

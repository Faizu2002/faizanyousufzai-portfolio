Local SEO

Local SEO built around relevance, local proof and useful pages.

I use local SEO to make a business easier to understand and easier to find in the places it actually serves. The work connects the website, Google Business Profile, local signals and measurement instead of treating them as separate checklists.

Start with the business and service area

Local SEO works best when the website clearly explains what the business does, where it serves customers and how someone can take the next step. I avoid creating thin city pages just to repeat a place name.

For businesses with a real local presence, the website and Business Profile should support the same facts. That includes services, hours, contact details and useful local proof.

Build pages around local search intent

A service query, a near-me query and an informational local question do not always need the same page. I map the search intent first, then decide whether an existing page should be improved or a new page is justified.

This keeps the site easier to crawl and reduces the risk of several pages competing for the same local query.

Measure visibility with business outcomes

Local rankings can vary by location, so I do not judge the whole strategy from one rank tracker screenshot. Search Console, Business Profile performance and lead data give a more useful picture.

Calls, forms, qualified visits, directions and visibility for relevant services matter more than publishing pages for every town on a map.

My local SEO workflow

I start by checking how the business is represented across the website and local search. The goal is to find mismatches before adding more pages. I review the main services, target areas, Google Business Profile setup, existing local landing pages, internal links and the queries already appearing in Search Console.

Next I connect each local intent to the best existing URL. If the site already has a strong service page, I improve that page instead of creating a near-duplicate city page. When a separate location page is justified, it should contain useful local details, service information, proof and a clear next step rather than swapped place names.

What I check for local SEO

  • Whether the business name, phone, hours and service details are consistent
  • Whether the primary services are clear on both the website and Business Profile
  • Whether local queries map to the right service or location page
  • Whether important pages are internally linked and crawlable
  • Whether citations and local links come from relevant sources
  • Whether reviews and local proof help users trust the business
  • Whether Search Console and lead data show real local progress

For the detailed process, use the Google Business Profile SEO guide, local keyword research guide and local citations guide. If the site has broader technical or indexing problems, I route those findings through the SEO Audit and Technical SEO hubs instead of mixing every issue into Local SEO.
